This map documents **visual simulation fixtures** added to the existing GLB renderer. It preserves the current studio panels and does not enable physical outputs. RGBW values are preview channels, not measured fixture commands or photometric data.

## Source limits

The imported models provide useful geometric datums, including seat, window, sidewall, footwell, and bin meshes. They do not include service-panel hardware, reading-lamp assemblies, photometric files, or passenger-door/exit nodes. The per-model manifest labels the cabin surfaces and fixture positions as authored simulation geometry. Consequently, all new emitters remain simulation additions and must not be read as aircraft-installed hardware.

Published airport-planning documents can establish exterior door stations and aircraft planning dimensions, but they do not establish a specific airline interior fit or connect those stations to the local coordinate origin of these authored GLBs. For example, Airbus's A350 Aircraft Characteristics planning manual lists passenger doors and emergency exits, and Boeing's 777-200LR/-300ER manual provides main-entry-door stations. Those sources do not supply the missing cabin-fixture schedules or a mesh-origin transform for these assets:

- [Airbus A350 Aircraft Characteristics — Airport and Maintenance Planning, July 2025](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/sites/g/files/jlcbta126/files/2025-07/AC_A350_20250715.pdf)
- [Boeing 777-200LR/-300ER Airport Planning manual, D6-58329-2 Rev G, December 2024](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/airports/acaps/777-200LR-300ER-F_Rev_G.pdf)

For an aircraft-fit preview, add an operator-approved cabin layout with door/exit stations, PSU and fixture coordinates, fixture identity, RGBW or dimming behavior, and beam/photometric data. Until then, keep labels and exports explicit that the light sources are simulated and their positions are model-relative estimates.

## Missing fixture placement audit

A scan of the 29 bundled GLBs (202,890 nodes) and the per-profile zone manifest found no named reading lamp or passenger-service-unit anchors, focused spotlight anchors, emergency-path fixture anchors, or passenger exit anchors for these four groups. Door-name matches were overhead-bin handles. The four unsupported groups remain unavailable in the bundled aircraft meshes.

The [Airbus A350 planning manual](https://aircraft.airbus.com/sites/g/files/jlcbta126/files/2025-07/AC_A350_20250715.pdf) records aircraft door positions. The bundled A350-900 GLB has no corresponding door anchors or verified mapping from the planning stations to its geometry. Those door positions cannot establish where exit signs or escape-path emitters belong in this model.

The comparison render test captures the supported decorative groups together at the Aurora and Sunset final keyframes in the A220-300 and A350-900. It checks that reading, spotlights, emergency, and exit options stay unavailable in both profiles.

A stock seat position, standard door station, typical light system arrangement, or fixture product listing is insufficient on its own. The four groups remain unavailable in the current bundled profiles.

The bundled QantasLink A220-300 profile uses the published 137-seat configuration as a reference. Its [profile data](../assets/aircraft-data/airlines/qantaslink-a220-300-137.json) identifies the metric seat positions and geometry as authored. Airbus door stations are measured from the aircraft nose, while this scene uses cabin-local coordinates. To place exit signs for this fit, the simulation needs the sign locations and orientations from a QantasLink-specific installation drawing or equivalent measured reference, plus corresponding landmarks in the bundled mesh to align the coordinates.

The [QantasLink seat map for the 137-seat A220-300](https://www.qantas.com/adobe/assets/urn%3Aaaid%3Aaem%3A0b52ece4-d1be-4286-8707-cb9580092c0b/original/as/airbus-a220-300-seat-map.pdf) identifies exit seating and the registrations to which that layout applies. Those references can help match a future installation drawing to the cabin fit. The map provides no sign mounting dimensions, lamp aim, or corresponding fixture landmarks in the mesh.

For the G650 family, the [NTSB report on test aircraft N652GD](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AAR1202.pdf) identifies the main entry door and forward and aft overwing exits on that aircraft. The [FAA draft G650/G650ER equipment list](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/draft_docs/mmel/MMEL_GVI_Rev_6_Draft.pdf) distinguishes cabin emergency lighting from passenger cabin illumination. Neither document locates the lighting fixtures or sign mounts for the G650ER interior represented in Studio.

Reading-light references must match the passenger service unit revision as well as the cabin layout. [Airbus's A220 Airspace announcement](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/newsroom/news/2023-06-a220-steps-into-the-airspace-family-with-new-cabin-design-features) identifies a redesigned Astronics unit paired with the new bins and ceiling. The Safran and Astronics references establish different product leads. The bundled QantasLink profile does not identify the installed unit revision or its mounting and aiming coordinates.

The A220-300 flight crew operating manual, volume 1, figure 01-02-43, illustrates the reading lamp within a passenger service unit under the overhead bins. It identifies the lamp separately from the reading switch and attendant-call light, but does not give installed row stations or lamp aim. For exits, the [Transport Canada A220 master minimum equipment list](https://wwwapps2.tc.gc.ca/saf-sec-sur/2/MEL-LEM/tcbbs/mmels/BD_500.pdf), ATA 33, describes the possibility of twin overwing exits sharing one sign. The [Airbus A220-300 rescue card](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/sites/default/files/2023-11/A220-300CFRC-MAST-ISS05-25Feb2022.pdf) locates the exits on the airframe. Together these references establish that counting or locating exits alone cannot determine the installed sign count or mounts for the QantasLink cabin.
